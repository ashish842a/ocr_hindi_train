Where the ResNet50-BiLSTM paper breaks, and how we beat it

An audit of Khan et al. (2026) against the IIIT-HW-Hindi corpus sitting on disk — eight documented gaps, six contributions, and a nine-row ablation grid the original paper does not have.

Headline finding
The architecture as described in the paper cannot train.

A stock ResNet50 downsamples by 32× in both dimensions. Measured on 4,000 real training images at input height 64, the encoder emits a median of 5 time steps for a median label of 6 characters. CTC loss is undefined whenever there are fewer frames than the label requires — which is true for 66.7% of this training set. Either the published pipeline is not the pipeline that was run, or two-thirds of the data was silently dropped.

Height is effectively constant at ~293 px, so width is the only informative dimension. Aspect spans a 6× range: fixed-size resizing destroys stroke detail, and fixed-width padding wastes roughly 60% of every batch. Bucketed batching plus per-sample CTC input lengths is mandatory — the paper discusses neither.

The undisclosed property that changes everything

Zero of 6,931 test word types are unseen in training. Zero of 8,603 validation types are unseen. All 95,430 labels are covered by the 11,030-word lexicon shipped with the dataset.

This benchmark is closed-vocabulary, and the paper does not say so. Its WER of 18% is measured on a task where the correct answer is always one of 11,030 candidates distributed alongside the images. A lexicon-constrained decoder is a large free win the paper leaves untaken — and the omission makes its Table 1 comparison against other work unsound.

Section 03 · eight gaps

Gap analysis

G1Fatal

CTC is mathematically infeasible for two-thirds of the data

Stock ResNet50 has total stride 32 (conv1 /2, maxpool /2, layer2 /2, layer3 /2, layer4 /2). Reshaping its output to a sequence yields T = W/32 frames. CTC requires T ≥ len(label) + adjacent repeats. Measured over 4,000 random training images:

Fix → replace stride-(2,2) with stride-(2,1) in layer3 and layer4 so height collapses but width survives at /4. Standard CRNN practice, zero cost, entirely absent from the paper. We keep the paper's configuration as ablation row A0 to document the defect empirically rather than merely assert it.
G2High

The attention mechanism as described is not a well-defined model

The paper specifies additive Bahdanau attention over BiLSTM outputs, then CTC loss and beam search over those same outputs. These are incompatible as written: Bahdanau attention presupposes an autoregressive decoder with its own output time axis, while CTC requires frame-synchronous, monotonic emission aligned to encoder frames. No equations, no tensor shapes, and — decisively — no ablation isolating attention. The paper's central claim that attention "substantially enhances word-level recognition" is supported by no reported experiment.

Fix → implement both defensible readings and compare them. (1) Transformer self-attention layers on top of the BiLSTM: frame count preserved, CTC stays valid. (2) True joint CTC–Attention — shared encoder, two heads, loss L = λ·L_ctc + (1−λ)·L_att, joint one-pass decoding. Reading (2) is our headline contribution: it is what the paper gestures at but never defines.
G3High

No Unicode normalisation — the metrics are not comparable to anything

The same rendered word is stored two different ways in the ground truth. Both boxes below are the identical grapheme:

ड़
U+095C
precomposed · 906 occurrences
ड़
U+0921 + U+093C
base + nukta · decomposed form

The train charset is 108 codepoints; NFC normalisation reduces it to 100 and rewrites 1,841 training labels. An identical prediction therefore scores differently depending on which encoding the reference happens to use. Separately, 32 characters occur fewer than 50 times and together cover just 0.14% of all character tokens — several are annotation noise rather than Hindi at all: ॓ grave accent, ॽ glottal stop, ऴ (a Dravidian letter), ॻ ॼ ॾ (Sindhi implosives).

Fix → NFC-normalise at manifest-build time, freeze an explicit charset, map the sub-50 tail to <unk>, and report CER both before and after normalisation to quantify how much of the published number is a tokenisation artefact.
G4Medium

Codepoint CER is the wrong metric for Devanagari

CER over codepoints scores a vowel sign as a unit equal to a consonant. Devanagari is read as akshara — grapheme clusters. Under codepoint CER these two errors cost exactly the same:

कि → की
one matra · barely visible
क → घ
a whole different consonant
Fix → report three metrics — codepoint CER, grapheme-cluster CER (Akshara Error Rate), and WER — and additionally train a variant whose CTC output vocabulary is akshara units rather than codepoints, so the model predicts what a reader actually perceives. Neither the metric nor the tokenisation appears in the paper.
G5High

The closed vocabulary is undisclosed and unexploited

Per §02: every test word type appears in training and every label is in the shipped lexicon. The paper mentions "a character-level language model" but never the lexicon and never the closure — leaving a large WER reduction unclaimed while simultaneously making its cross-paper comparison unsound.

Fix → report four decoding regimes side by side — greedy, unconstrained beam, beam + character n-gram shallow fusion, and lexicon-constrained beam over a prefix trie of the 11,030 words — plus an open-vocabulary protocol that holds out word types from training to measure genuine generalisation. Unconstrained numbers always lead; closed-vocabulary results get their own clearly labelled column.
G6Medium

Writer-disjointness is never analysed

The splits are writer-disjoint and the test set is dominated by two of its three writers, yet the paper reports one aggregate number with no variance and no per-writer breakdown. Generalising to an unseen hand is the difficulty of this benchmark, and it goes unmeasured.

Fix → per-writer CER/WER with bootstrap confidence intervals, leave-one-writer-out cross-validation across the seven training writers, and a real modelling contribution: a writer-adversarial branch (gradient reversal on a writer-ID classifier) pushing the encoder toward writer-invariant features, benchmarked against classical slant/slope normalisation.
G7Medium

There is no reproducibility surface

No code, no seeds, no training curves, no parameter count, no latency, no confidence intervals, contradictory hyperparameters, contradictory headline numbers, and a comparison table whose baselines were copied from other papers rather than re-run on a matched split.

Fix → fixed seeds, released code, deterministic manifests, per-epoch logs, params and latency columns, 1,000-resample bootstrap CIs, and re-implementation of the CRNN/VGG-BiLSTM baseline on this exact split so the comparison is apples to apples.
G8Medium

Variable-width batching is unaddressed

With aspect ratios from 1.06 to 6.64, padding to a fixed width without passing per-sample lengths to CTCLoss corrupts the loss, and padding to batch-max wastes most of the compute. The paper says only "resize to constant height preserving aspect ratio" and stops there.

Fix → aspect-ratio bucketed sampler, right-padding with an explicit input_lengths tensor, and padding-aware masking in both the BiLSTM (pack_padded_sequence) and the attention layers.

Leftover from the earlier layout

An empty code/ folder currently sits at this repo root from before the project was restructured. Git doesn't track empty directories, so it's invisible to version control and harmless — delete it whenever, or leave it until src/ and scripts/ land.

Portability rules

  • utils/device.py resolves cuda → mps → cpu and gates AMP to CUDA only.
  • Every path comes from config. The notebook overrides two keys and nothing else.
  • num_workers derived from os.cpu_count(), forced to 2 on Colab.
  • Checkpoints every epoch carry full RNG state, so a Colab timeout resumes cleanly — non-negotiable against the 12-hour limit.
  • Zero notebook-only logic. The notebook shells out to scripts/train.py; nothing is defined inside it.
  • Batch size and gradient accumulation are config-driven, so a 16 GB T4 and a local 4090 run the same experiment at the same effective batch size.
Environment constraints on this machine

This Mac cannot train. Intel i7-1068NG7, Intel Iris Plus graphics, 16 GB RAM — no CUDA, and MPS requires Apple Silicon. Use it for data preparation, analysis, plotting and writing only.

Python 3.14.7 is installed and PyTorch ships no wheels for it. A Python 3.11 or 3.12 virtualenv is required for anything importing torch. scripts/prepare_data.py is deliberately written against the standard library plus Pillow, so the audit stage runs anywhere.

Data movement. 1.8 GB compressed, ~3.5 GB extracted. Upload the tarball to Drive once and extract to Colab's local disk each session — reading 95k small JPEGs off mounted Drive is pathologically slow. The P0 LMDB pack reduces this to a single ~3 GB file.
